# Getting help

## Help with a repository

Open an issue in the repository the problem belongs to, with the issue form that fits it best; in repositories that use the organization's forms, only people with write access can open a blank issue. The [contributing guide](https://github.com/KingfishProject/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) explains how issues and pull requests work in Kingfish repositories. Issues in a public repository are public, so keep anything about a source out of them, and report a security problem as the [security policy](https://github.com/KingfishProject/.github/blob/main/SECURITY.md) says instead.

## Help with Kingfish's journalism

Reader questions go through the pages kingfish.la publishes for them, not through GitHub:

- **An error in a published story:** the [Corrections Policy](https://kingfish.la/legal/ethics/#corrections) says where to report it.
- **A tip or a document:** start at [kingfish.la/tips](https://kingfish.la/tips/). Before you send anything sensitive, read [Contacting us and sending tips](https://kingfish.la/legal/privacy-policy/#contacting-us-and-sending-tips) in the Privacy Policy.
- **Republishing a story:** the [Republishing Policy](https://kingfish.la/legal/republishing-policy/) gives the license terms and a credit line that meets them.
- **Information Kingfish holds about you:** the [Your rights](https://kingfish.la/legal/privacy-policy/#your-rights) section of the Privacy Policy says what you can ask for and where to send the request.
- **A barrier on the site:** the [Accessibility Statement](https://kingfish.la/legal/a11y/#if-something-doesnt-work-for-you) says how to report it.
- **A story that falls short of Kingfish's standards:** the Editorial Policy's [Questions and accountability](https://kingfish.la/legal/editorial-policy/#questions-and-accountability) section says whom to tell.
